Personal safety

One of the
safest.

Short version: Montréal is one of the safest major cities in North America. Violent crime is rare; the real risks are petty theft and — in November — ice.

The honest picture. Montréal has one of the lowest crime rates of Canada's big cities. Random attacks on strangers are uncommon. What actually happens to visitors is opportunistic petty theft — not violence.
01

The actual risks

Petty theft

  • Pickpocketing in crowds and busy metro stations (esp. Berri-UQAM).
  • Phones lifted off café tables and bar counters.
  • Bags taken off the back of a chair while you eat.

Cross-body bag, phone in a front pocket, nothing left unattended on the table, and you've handled ~95% of it.

The bigger hazard: ice. Slipping on glazed sidewalks sends more visitors to urgent care than crime does. Grippy shoes, short "penguin" steps, and use the Underground City when it's nasty.
02

Where to skip

Areas with higher crime

mostly not near you

These have higher crime than the tourist core — and you have basically no reason to be in them anyway. Even then they're generally fine by day:

  • Hochelaga-Maisonneuve (east; gentrifying but uneven)
  • Saint-Michel and Montréal-Nord (northeast)
  • Eastern Ville-Marie / Downtown East — you'll see some street homelessness; more a "keep normal awareness" than a danger zone

Your whole itinerary — Old Montréal, downtown, Plateau, Mile End, Little Italy, Little Burgundy — sits in the safe, busy, well-lit parts.

03

Night-out smarts

  • Watch your drinks — don't leave them unattended at the bar.
  • Stick to well-lit, busy streets late; skip cutting through empty parks after dark.
  • Use official taxis or a rideshare app late at night rather than anyone soliciting rides.
  • Montréal ranks among the safest big cities for solo women — but the usual awareness still applies.
04

If something happens

Emergency: 911 (police / fire / ambulance — English or French).
Save your travel-insurance line, and note major hospitals accept walk-ins (MUHC / CHUM downtown). Non-residents pay out of pocket, so insurance matters.
