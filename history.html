The backstory

1642
to now.

A quick version of how a missionary fort on a river island became North America's most French-flavored city — handy context for what you're walking through.

before 1535
First peoples

Hochelaga

Long before Europeans, the St. Lawrence Iroquoians lived in a village called Hochelaga at the foot of the mountain. In Kanien'kéha (Mohawk), the place is Tiohtià:ke — a name you'll still see around the city today.

1535
Arrival

Cartier names the mountain

French explorer Jacques Cartier reaches Hochelaga, climbs the hill, and calls it Mont Royal — "royal mountain." That name, softened over time, becomes "Montréal."

1611
Fur trade

Champlain's trading post

Samuel de Champlain sets up a fur-trading spot at Place Royale — the exact site where the Pointe-à-Callière museum stands today, in Old Montréal.

May 17, 1642
Founding

Ville-Marie is born

Paul de Chomedey de Maisonneuve and Jeanne Mance found a small Catholic mission, Ville-Marie, dedicated to the Virgin Mary. Mance opens the first hospital — one of the earliest in North America. This fort is the city's beginning.

1701
Peace

The Great Peace of Montréal

A landmark treaty between the French and dozens of First Nations ends decades of conflict — and lets the fur-trade town grow into a real city.

1760
Conquest

The British take over

Montréal surrenders to the British, ending French rule in New France. The French language, faith, and culture stay put — the tension and blend you still feel today starts here.

1920s–30s
Nightlife

"Sin City"

While the US ran Prohibition, Montréal's bars, jazz clubs and cabarets roared — earning it a wild-nightlife reputation that never fully left.

1967–76
Modern boom

Expo 67, the Metro & the Olympics

The World's Fair (Expo 67), a new subway, and the 1976 Summer Olympics put Montréal on the global stage. The Olympic Stadium's debt famously wasn't paid off until 2006.

1960s→
Identity

The Quiet Revolution

A wave of reform and rising Québécois pride reshapes the province — and cements French as the working language of the city you're visiting.

today
Now

A bilingual festival city

~1.8 million people, officially French-speaking, widely bilingual, and packed with food, art, and festivals year-round. Old and new, French and North American, all at once.
