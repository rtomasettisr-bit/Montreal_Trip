01

The mental math

Rule of thumb: knock ~30% off any Canadian price to get roughly what it costs you in USD. A $100 CAD dinner ≈ $71 USD. A $50 CAD cab ≈ $35 USD.

Quick converter (CAD → USD)

  • $10 CAD ≈ $7 USD

  • $100 CAD ≈ $71 USD
  • $200 CAD ≈ $142 USD
02

Paying for things

Tap everything

Montréal is basically cashless — tap/contactless works nearly everywhere. Carry a little cash only for spots like Schwartz's and the bagel shops.

Use a no-foreign-transaction-fee card so you get the real rate and skip ~3% in fees. Tell your bank you'll be in Canada so nothing gets flagged.

"Would you like to pay in USD?"

When a card terminal offers to charge you in US dollars, always choose CAD. That "dynamic currency conversion" bakes in a worse rate — let your own bank do the conversion.

ATMs > airport/hotel exchange

If you want cash, pull CAD from a bank ATM rather than exchanging bills at a kiosk — better rate, fewer fees.

03

Tax & tipping

Tax isn't in the sticker price. Québec adds 14.975% at the register (5% federal GST + 9.975% provincial QST) — call it ~15%. The $18 cocktail rings up closer to $21 CAD before tip.

Tipping, roughly US-style

  • Restaurants / bars: 15–20% (many terminals suggest it — note it's often calculated on the post-tax total).
  • Taxis / rideshare: ~10–15%.
  • Coffee counters: optional; round up or a dollar.
  • Hotel housekeeping / bellhop: a few dollars.
