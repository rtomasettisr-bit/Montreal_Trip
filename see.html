Early November is a cold-weather city — this leans toward warm interiors, markets, one killer view, and a couple's soak on the water. Pick what fits the day.

Just for two

Mount Royal — Kondiaronk Lookout

the mountainbest viewfree

The postcard skyline shot, from the big terrace on the city's namesake hill. Walk up (some stairs) or take the #11 bus. Do it in daylight — sunset's around 4:40. Warm up inside the chalet.

Old Port & Rue Saint-Paul

Vieux-Montréalwalkfree

Cobblestone streets, the waterfront, and the prettiest part of the city on foot. Bundle up — it's exposed off the river. Best at dusk when the lamps come on.

La Grande Roue de Montréal

Old Portferris wheel$29.50/adult

Canada's tallest observation wheel (60 m), right on the water — the cabins are heated, so it works in November. An 18-minute ride, best after dark when the city lights up. Student rate ~$24.

Street art — Plateau & Mile End

Boul. Saint-Laurentmuralsfree

The Main and the side streets north of it are wall-to-wall murals from the summer MURAL festival. Great photo-walk between bagels and Mount Royal.

Notre-Dame Basilica

Old Montréalmust-see~$16 CAD

The cobalt-and-gold interior is the single best indoor sight in the city — worth it even if churches aren't your thing. The evening AURA light-and-sound show turns it into something else entirely (separate ticket).

Montréal Museum of Fine Arts

Golden Square Milefree if 25 & under~$24 · 26+

Rainy-day proof and genuinely great — classical to contemporary across several pavilions. Admission is free for anyone 25 or under, so if that's you two, it's a no-brainer. 26+ is ~$24 (half-price Wednesday evenings). Budget 2–3 hours; a big temporary exhibition costs extra.

Pointe-à-Callière

Old Montréalhistoryticketed · book online

Archaeology museum built directly on top of the 1642 founding site — you walk through the original fort foundations underground. The best way to feel the city's age. The backstory →

Underground City (RÉSO)

Downtownbad-weather planfree

~33 km of connected downtown passages — shops, food, metro — all without a coat. Your insurance policy for a cold, wet afternoon. Signage is spotty, so it's half the fun to just wander.

Jean-Talon Market

Little Italypartly indoorfree entry

Big public market — cheese, charcuterie, Québec produce, oysters, cider. Graze your way through and call it lunch.

Atwater Market

Little Burgundy · canalprettier / smallerfree entry

Compact indoor market on the Lachine Canal — great for edible souvenirs on the way out of town, then a stroll through Little Burgundy's boutiques.

Bota Bota — spa-sur-l'eau

Old Portcouple's move$$$

A Nordic spa on a moored boat: sauna → cold plunge → hot pools, with old-port views and steam rising off the water in the cold. Book the thermal circuit (add massages if you're celebrating). The best warm-up of the whole trip.

AURA at Notre-Dame

Old Montréalnight show$$

An immersive light-and-sound show inside the basilica that lights up the whole interior. A genuinely memorable date-night — book a slot in advance.

Closed for the season. La Ronde (the amusement park) wraps its season in late October — a final Halloween "Fright Fest" weekend runs to about Nov 1, then it's done. Most terraces and river cruises are shut too. That's fine — this is an indoor-culture-and-food weekend, and it's better for it.
