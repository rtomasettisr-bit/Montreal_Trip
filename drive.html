Getting there

Connecticut
to Canada.

From Fairfield County it's roughly a 330-mile, ~6-hour haul before the border. Two solid routes — pick by traffic.

01

The route

Option A — through Vermont

I-91 N → I-89 N~6 hrs

North up I-91 through Hartford into Vermont, then I-89 to the Highgate Springs / St-Armand crossing, onto Autoroute 10 into downtown. The prettier drive.

Option B — through New York

I-87 "The Northway"~6 hrs

West to the Hudson, then straight up I-87 through the Adirondacks to the Champlain / Lacolle crossing — Canada's busiest, so check the wait before you commit.

Check live routing the morning of. Times swing with weather and border queues. Once you cross, signs flip to French — "Sortie" = exit, "Arrêt" = stop.
02

Parking & the car

Park it and forget it

Book a hotel with a garage or reserve a lot on ClicknPark. Then leave the car all weekend — the old town + metro cover everything. Watch for orange snow-removal signs; a ticket beats a tow.

Snow tires aren't required for out-of-province visitors, but drive to conditions — first flurries are possible in early November.

03

Pack for it

  • Layers — highs ~47°F, colder at night, dark by ~4:40pm.
  • Waterproof shell — early November leans rainy, maybe first flurries.
  • Grippy shoes — icy sidewalks are the real hazard; falls, not crime.
  • Passports, meds, chargers, a good playlist.
Get travel medical insurance. Canada's system won't cover you, and a hospital visit as a non-resident runs into the thousands. Cheap peace of mind for a road trip.
