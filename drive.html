MTL · getting there
by car
The drive

Connecticut
to Canada.

From Fairfield County it's roughly a 330-mile, ~6-hour haul before the border. Two solid routes — pick by traffic.

01

The route

Option A — through Vermont

I-91 N → I-89 N~6 hrs

North up I-91 through Hartford and into Vermont, then I-89 to the Highgate Springs / St-Armand border crossing, onto Autoroute 10 into downtown. The prettier drive.

Option B — through New York

I-87 "The Northway"~6 hrs

West to the Hudson, then straight up I-87 through the Adirondacks to the Champlain / Lacolle crossing — Canada's busiest, so check the wait before you commit.

Check live routing the morning of. Times swing with weather and border queues. Whichever way you go, street signs flip to French once you cross — "Sortie" = exit, "Arrêt" = stop.
02

The border

What to bring

passport

Bring a valid passport each. It's not technically the only option, but Canada's border agency calls it the only reliable one — anything else (birth certificate + license, enhanced ID) risks slow secondary screening. US citizens need no visa for stays under 180 days.

Have car registration and insurance handy, and know what you're carrying (no cannabis across the border either way — still federally illegal to import/export).

At the booth

Windows down, sunglasses off, radio off, phones away. Answer plainly: where you're going (Montréal), how long (the weekend), why (tourism). Be calm and boring — that's the whole trick.

Heads up: a DUI on record can get you turned away — look into it before you go if it applies.

03

Once you're there

Parking

Book a hotel with a garage or reserve a lot on ClicknPark. Then leave the car put — the old town and the metro cover everything. Watch for orange snow-removal signs; a ticket beats a tow.

Money & phones

Everything takes tap; you barely need cash (a little for Schwartz's / bagels). Tell your bank you're in Canada, or use a no-foreign-fee card. Check your phone plan for a Canada day-pass so you're not roaming blind.

04

Pack for it

Layers — highs ~8°C / mid-40s°F, colder at night, dark by ~4:40pm.
Waterproof shell — early November leans rainy, maybe first flurries.
Grippy shoes — icy sidewalks are the real hazard; falls, not crime.
Passports, meds, chargers, a good playlist.

Get travel medical insurance. Canada's system won't cover you, and a hospital visit as a non-resident runs into the thousands. Cheap peace of mind for a road trip.
