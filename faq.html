Quick answers

The obvious
questions.

Do we need passports?
Yes — bring a valid passport each. Other documents technically work but risk a slow secondary screening, and there's no visa needed for a weekend. Border details →
Is it safe?
Very — it's one of the safest major cities in North America. Violent crime is rare; petty theft (phones, pickpockets in crowds) is the real risk, and icy sidewalks are the bigger hazard in November. Your whole itinerary is in the safe, busy areas. Full safety page →
Do they speak English?
French is the official language, but Montréal is very bilingual — especially in restaurants, hotels, and tourist areas, English is widely spoken. A friendly "bonjour / hi" is the local greeting. Knowing "merci" and "s'il vous plaît" goes a long way.
How cold will it be? What do we pack?
Early November highs run around 47°F, colder at night, with a decent chance of rain and maybe first flurries. Pack warm layers, a waterproof shell, and shoes with grip for icy sidewalks. It's dark by ~4:40pm, so plan outdoor stuff for midday. Packing list →
What's the deal with money?
Everything's in Canadian dollars, and $1 USD ≈ $1.41 CAD — so knock ~30% off any price to see it in USD. Tap/contactless works nearly everywhere; carry a little cash for the deli and bagel shops. Use a no-foreign-fee card. Money page →
Do we need the car once we're there?
No. Park it at the hotel and leave it — Old Montréal is walkable and the metro covers the rest. Driving downtown just means fighting one-way streets and paying for parking. Parking →
Is early November actually a good time?
For this kind of trip, yes — it's shoulder season, so hotels are cheaper and crowds are thin, and your dates land inside MTLàTABLE restaurant week. The trade-off is short, cold, sometimes-wet days, so it's an indoor-culture-and-food weekend rather than a patio one.
Do we need reservations?
For dinners, yes — book a week out, and earlier for the big rooms (Le Club Chasse et Pêche) during restaurant week. The hidden cocktail bars don't take reservations; just go a little early. Bota Bota (the spa) — book the thermal circuit ahead.
How do we get around?
Walk within neighborhoods; take the metro between them (clean, cheap, warm — grab a multi-day OPUS card). Rideshare and taxis work late at night. The Underground City connects a lot of downtown without going outside.
Will our phones work?
Yes, but check your plan — add a Canada day-pass or an eSIM so you're not roaming blind. Wifi is everywhere. Tell your bank you'll be in Canada so your cards don't get flagged.
Weed is legal there, right?
Cannabis is legal in Canada for adults (21+ in Québec, bought at the government SQDC), but you cannot carry it across the border in either direction — that's still a federal offense both ways. Buy and finish it in Canada, or skip it.
How long is the drive?
Roughly 6 hours (~330 miles) from Fairfield County before the border — via Vermont (I-91→I-89) or New York (I-87). Leave in the morning to cross in daylight, and check live routing the day-of. Route →
