Crossing over

The border,
demystified.

It's genuinely quick and routine — a few minutes at a booth. Here's exactly what to have ready so you're the easy car in line.

01

What to bring

Passports — plain and simple

bring these

Each of you needs a valid passport. It isn't technically the only option, but Canada's border agency calls it the only reliable one — birth-certificate-plus-license combos and enhanced IDs work but risk slow secondary screening. US citizens need no visa for stays under 180 days.

For the car

  • Driver's license (your US license is fine to drive in Canada as a visitor)
  • Vehicle registration
  • Proof of insurance
02

At the booth

Be the boring car

Windows down, sunglasses off, radio off, phones away. Answer plainly: where (Montréal), how long (the weekend), why (tourism). No jokes, no over-explaining. The whole thing usually takes a couple of minutes.

03

Don't bring

  • Cannabis — still illegal to carry across the border either direction, even where it's legal on both sides.
  • Excess alcohol/tobacco — know the personal duty-free limits; declare over them.
  • Undeclared gifts or big purchases — coming home, declare what you bought in Canada.
One real gotcha. A DUI/DWI on record can get you turned away at the Canadian border. If that applies to either of you, look into it (and any waivers) well before the trip.
Speed it up. The free ArriveCAN app (official source only) can pre-fill your info. NEXUS lanes are the car version of Global Entry if you're frequent crossers — but overkill for one weekend.
